# C4 oral preparation — Module 4

Working answers for the four questions in the [current Module 4 question bank](https://sethfraden.github.io/Phys39F26-course/labs/lab-04/#c4-oral-questions-open-loop-tec-calibration). Explain these in your own words at the apparatus. Numerical graph results below are **provisional** until Part 3 is revalidated under the revised one-minute criterion.

1. **Two independent protections.** The Arduino checks the average of 1000 raw thermistor ADC samples against a named 60 °C threshold each loop; above it, both H-bridge PWM outputs go to zero while serial temperature and shutdown status continue. The normally closed thermal switch is physically in series with the TEC power path and can open without Arduino cooperation. In the instructor-present test, a temporary 30 °C software limit was crossed: the recorded temperature reached 30.67 °C, the GUI reported `Safety=SHUTDOWN`, and both firmware-reported PWM outputs were zero. The source was restored to 60 °C afterward. This establishes the *reported firmware behavior*, not a separate measured pin voltage or a test of the hardware switch opening.

2. **Graph and steady state.** Use signed PWM `u`: HEAT positive, COOL negative, and both fitted slopes positive. Preliminary fits over 0–45 HEAT and −96–0 COOL give `m_h=0.4954` and `m_c=0.1414 °C/PWM count`, so `r=m_h/m_c=3.5035`. The points are close to straight lines (`R²≈0.999` each), though slight curvature or data changes are possible. The **current** professor-defined steady test is: estimate the step-response `τ`, wait about `3τ`, then observe another minute; net drift over that minute must be no larger than ordinary short-term wiggles. Our old 20-second averaging windows alone do not establish this, so mention the [audit](../module_notes/module_04_open_loop_tec.md#revised-steady-state-protocol-audit) and any later repeats honestly. H4/C4 are instructor-approved 10–45 °C course-range exceptions, not hidden points.

3. **Why the slopes differ.** Reversing current reverses the Peltier heat transport but not `I²R` Joule heating. At the object face, the two effects add in HEAT and Joule heat partly cancels Peltier cooling in COOL. The passive term `−G(T−T₀)` carries heat out of an above-room-temperature block and into a below-room-temperature block; it opposes both excursions. Approximately symmetric conduction alone does not create unequal slope magnitudes. In the simplified signed-duty model `Q̇_TEC=dQ̇_P+|d|Q̇_J` and `d=u/255`, so `m_h=(Q̇_P+Q̇_J)/(255G)` and `m_c=(Q̇_P−Q̇_J)/(255G)`. The provisional measured ratio implies `Q̇_J/Q̇_P=(r−1)/(r+1)=0.5559` within this model, not a direct heat-flow measurement.

4. **Laird sheet and comparison.** In the CP14-127-045 **27 °C hot-side** table: module resistance `R_M=1.50 Ω`, maximum current `I_max=8.6 A` (listed as `I @ ΔTmax`), maximum cooling heat pump `Q_c,max=71.3 W` at `ΔT=0`, and maximum no-load temperature difference `ΔT_max=70.5 °C` at `Qc=0` (Tark's page lists `Q_c,max=71.32 W`). Then `Q̇_J,max=½I_max²R_M=½×8.6²×1.50=55.47 W`, `Q̇_P,max=Q_c,max+Q̇_J,max=126.77 W`, and `r_Laird,max=(Q̇_P,max+Q̇_J,max)/(Q̇_P,max−Q̇_J,max)=1+I_max²R_M/Q_c,max=2.556`. Compare that ratio to a **revalidated** measured `r≈3.50`, rather than assuming equality (measured is ≈1.37× the manufacturer value). PWM duty 1 only means continuous H-bridge drive; it does not establish `I=I_max`, because supply settings, bridge and wire drops, TEC resistance, operating temperatures, and current dependence all matter. Because the symmetric model gives `r→1` below `I_max`, a measured `r>2.56` also signals the model's idealizations (half-Joule split, single symmetric `G`), not a simple current deficit.

Quick self-check: If the examiner asks why PWM Joule heating is linear in duty, write `⟨I⟩=DI` and `⟨I²⟩=DI²`, **not** `⟨I²⟩=(DI)²`. If asked about steady state, say net heat flow is zero while individual heat flows generally are not.
